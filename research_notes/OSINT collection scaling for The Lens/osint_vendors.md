# Commercial OSINT / Social-Listening / Physical-Security Threat-Intelligence Vendors for a Solo Crisis-Monitoring Consultancy

## What exactly do enterprise OSINT/PSI vendors monitor, and can a small buyer get access?

### Takeaway
All twelve enterprise-tier vendors researched (Dataminr, Babel Street, Recorded Future, Flashpoint, ZeroFox, Liferaft, ShadowDragon, Factal, Signal AI, Brandwatch, Meltwater, Talkwalker) monitor broad combinations of news, social media, messaging apps (Telegram and similar), forums, and in several cases deep/dark web sources — but nearly all of them are sales-led, quote-only, and priced well above what a solo consultancy can realistically pay (commonly $10K–$500K+/year), with Talkwalker and Brandwatch the only ones with any published low-tier number, and none confirmed to have a true self-serve signup.

### Cited Findings
- **Dataminr**: Pricing is "$50,000–$200,000+ annually" with first-year TCO of "$75,000–$250,000+" including implementation; one listing cites an entry point "starts at $500 per user/month." Described as "primarily an enterprise-level solution with a budget threshold of $50,000+ annually," explicitly noting "companies with limited budgets or smaller teams might find the platform's cost prohibitive." — [ITQlick Dataminr Pricing](https://www.itqlick.com/dataminr/pricing)
- **Babel Street**: Monitors "social media, news outlets, government feeds, and the deep and dark web," supports "over 200 languages," and accesses "hard-to-reach, in-country data sources across 95+ countries." Offers API integration — "insights are easily integrated into existing systems using APIs." Positioned toward national-security/government and enterprise "decision-makers" needing persistent threat monitoring (identity risk, strategic threat, vendor risk intelligence). No public dollar pricing found anywhere searched — a third-party comparison page explicitly states "dollar pricing was not published in the official materials reviewed." — [Babel Street OSINT Streams](https://www.babelstreet.com/babel-street-data/osint-streams); [DigitalStakeout Babel Street comparison](https://www.digitalstakeout.com/compare/babel-street-alternative)
- **Recorded Future**: Single-module entry pricing is "$40,000 to $60,000" annually; mid-market (3–5 modules) "$75,000 to $200,000" ACV; full enterprise suite "$250,000 to $500,000+." Stays quote-only ("the bundled-quote model preserves their commercial leverage"), effectively locking out solo buyers who lack procurement leverage. — [UnderDefense Recorded Future Pricing Guide](https://underdefense.com/blog/recorded-future-pricing-guide/)
- **Flashpoint (Ignite)**: Monitors "global social media, messaging apps," "defense forums, and illicit communities," over 1 billion indexed news articles, 975 million+ illicit forum posts, and 69 billion+ stolen credentials, across "3.6 petabytes" of data. Offers API integration via a "robust API ecosystem" supporting STIX/TAXII standards. Target customers are midsize-to-large enterprises and public sector (cited customers: DOD, Microsoft, Wells Fargo, Kaiser Permanente); sales-led with "Get a Demo"/"Talk to Sales" CTAs and no visible self-serve tier. — [Flashpoint Ignite](https://flashpoint.io/ignite/)
- **ZeroFox**: Median buyer pays "$45,604 per year" (range $19,790–$76,889, n=38 purchases); by tier: Essential (10–25 assets) $30,000–$60,000/yr, Advanced (25–100 assets) $75,000–$150,000/yr, Enterprise (100+ assets) $200,000–$500,000+/yr. Has an "Essential" tier aimed at smaller organizations but entry still runs $30K–$50K/year, positioning it for mid-market/enterprise rather than true small businesses. Costs scale with number of protected assets (domains, social accounts, executives). — [Vendr ZeroFox pricing](https://www.vendr.com/marketplace/zerofox)
- **Liferaft**: Acquired by Securitas — a binding agreement was signed **February 3, 2026**, with the deal expected to close in H1 2026; as of the announcement the acquisition had **not yet completed**. No name/brand change for the Liferaft product was mentioned in the acquisition release; Securitas plans to "apply Liferaft's threat-intelligence capabilities and proprietary IP across our client base," implying the Liferaft brand continues for now. No rebrand to "Netwatcher" was found in any source searched — that name does not appear in connection with Liferaft in the sources retrieved (treat as unconfirmed/gap). — [PR Newswire: Securitas acquires Liferaft](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/securitas-acquires-liferaft-a-leading-saas-threat-intelligence-platform-provider-302678257.html)
- **ShadowDragon (SocialNet/Horizon)**: Confirmed primarily a government/law-enforcement sales model — cited example: "DOJ Drug Enforcement Agency signs $29M contract for ShadowDragon Horizon/Socialnet solution" (a multi-year, multi-agency-scale deal, not indicative of small-buyer pricing). Company reported ~$6.6M revenue with a 44-person team in 2023. No per-seat or entry-tier dollar pricing was found in any source searched; product pages for specific pricing (SOTA2 listing) returned no content. — [Orange Slices AI: DEA ShadowDragon contract](https://orangeslices.ai/doj-drug-enforcement-agency-inks-29m-contract-for-shadowdragon-horizon-socialnet-solution/); [Getlatka: ShadowDragon revenue](https://getlatka.com/companies/shadowdragon.io/competitors)
- **Factal**: No specific public pricing figures were found in any source searched (searches returned unrelated "crisis PR cost" and "crisis communications pricing" articles, not Factal's own SaaS pricing). Factal was originally founded (2018) by the Breaking News app founders to deliver "real-time news and incident alerts to companies," suggesting a B2B/corporate-risk target customer, but current (2026) pricing and buyer-size accessibility could not be confirmed. — [GeekWire: Factal launch](https://www.geekwire.com/2018/breaking-news-app-founders-target-corporations-new-incident-alert-startup-factal/)
- **Signal AI**: Searches returned only generic pricing-aggregator pages (Capterra, GetApp, SoftwareAdvice) with no extracted dollar figures; none were fetched for full content due to tool-call budget. Treat pricing as **unconfirmed** — known in the industry as an enterprise media-intelligence/monitoring platform, but no cited figure available.
- **Brandwatch**: Uses custom/quote-based pricing across three tiers (Consumer Intelligence, Social Media Management, Influencer Marketing) with "no free trial or free tier." Third-party cost estimates: **$800–$5,000/month**, with median annual contract around **$80,000/year** (5 verified buyer reports). — [Costbench: Brandwatch pricing](https://costbench.com/software/social-media-management/brandwatch/)
- **Meltwater**: Starter plan begins around **$10,000/year**; real-world contracts cluster near **$25,800/year** (126 tracked purchases); typical range **$10,000–$130,000+/year**, with "$100,000+ for global enterprise deployments." No self-serve: "no free trial, no free plan, and no monthly billing — contracts are annual with a 60-day auto-renewal cancellation window," and the public pricing page routes to a sales call rather than showing prices. — [Octolens: Meltwater pricing 2026](https://octolens.com/blog/meltwater-pricing)
- **Talkwalker**: Entry "Listening" plan is **$9,000/year**, aimed at "individuals and small teams," offering "the best data coverage on social and web" with dashboards and alerts. Higher tiers (Analytics, Research) are custom-quoted; teams of 50+ users must go through sales. No free trial. This is the lowest confirmed entry price point among the enterprise social-listening/OSINT vendors researched. — [SpotSaaS: Talkwalker pricing 2026](https://www.spotsaas.com/product/talkwalker/pricing)

### Inferences
- Of the twelve named vendors, **Talkwalker ($9,000/yr)** and possibly **Meltwater (~$10,000/yr nominal Starter, though real deals run higher)** are the only ones with a quasi-accessible entry price for a solo/small consultancy — everything else (Dataminr, Babel Street, Recorded Future, Flashpoint, ZeroFox, ShadowDragon, Brandwatch) clusters at $30K–$500K+/year and is explicitly enterprise/government sales-led.
- Vendors with API access confirmed (Babel Street, Flashpoint) are built for integration into a platform like The Lens, but their price floors make that integration financially impractical for a solo operation.
- Dark-web/forum/Telegram coverage (Flashpoint, Babel Street, ShadowDragon) is concentrated in the highest-priced, most enterprise/government-oriented tier of vendors — none of these offer a meaningfully discounted path for small buyers.
- Liferaft's acquisition by Securitas (agreement signed Feb 3, 2026, not yet closed as of the source date) means its roadmap, pricing, and even branding could change in 2026; no rebrand to "Netwatcher" is confirmed, so the user's assumption should be treated as unverified.

### Gaps
- No confirmed self-serve/online-signup pricing tier (as opposed to "contact sales") was found for Dataminr, Babel Street, Recorded Future, Flashpoint, ZeroFox, ShadowDragon, Factal, Signal AI, Brandwatch, or Meltwater — all appear to require a sales conversation, which may itself be a barrier for a one-person shop even where a "low" tier nominally exists.
- Signal AI and Factal pricing could not be established at all from the searches run; a follow-up search specifically on vendor pricing-page content (not aggregator listicles) is needed.
- No source confirmed whether Liferaft has rebranded as "Netwatcher" in 2026 — this should be verified directly against Liferaft's/Securitas's current website before publishing, since no evidence of that name was found.
- ShadowDragon's actual list/seat pricing (as opposed to multi-million-dollar government contract examples) was not found; it may not publish any tier pricing to the public at all.
- Babel Street's own published price list (if any) could not be located; all evidence is about product capability, not cost.

## Do any of these vendors specifically emphasize African coverage or multilingual/local-language African media?

### Takeaway

### Cited Findings
- Babel Street claims coverage "across 95+ countries" and "over 200 languages," which would nominally include African countries/languages, but no source found explicitly names African-language media, Africa-specific data partnerships, or regional offices. — [Babel Street OSINT Streams](https://www.babelstreet.com/babel-street-data/osint-streams)

AI content moderation covers fewer than 20" — which, while not about any specific named vendor, supports treating claims of broad multilingual African coverage from any of these platforms with skepticism absent explicit vendor confirmation. — [Rising Voices: Africa has 2,000 languages](https://rising.globalvoices.org/blog/2026/04/20/africa-has-2000-languages-ai-content-moderation-covers-fewer-than-20/)

### Inferences
- None of the researched vendors appear to differentiate on African-language/local-media coverage as a selling point; this is likely a genuine gap in the commercial OSINT market relevant to Afrilens's stated focus, rather than a research-coverage miss, but could not be fully confirmed without vendor-by-vendor product-page deep dives beyond the tool-call budget.

### Gaps
- Direct confirmation (or denial) of African-language source coverage per vendor was not obtained for Recorded Future, Flashpoint, ZeroFox, Dataminr, ShadowDragon, Factal, Signal AI, Brandwatch, Meltwater, or Talkwalker — each vendor's own regional-coverage documentation would need to be checked directly (not found via the general searches run).
- No African-specific social-listening/OSINT vendor (e.g., regional players) was identified in this research pass; that would require a dedicated search focused on "Africa OSINT vendor" / "African social listening company," which was outside this task's assigned scope.

## Are there lower-cost / API-first alternatives aimed at developers, with real pricing at useful (non-trial) volume?

### Takeaway
Yes — several developer-facing news/data APIs (Webz.io, NewsCatcher, Event Registry/NewsAPI.ai, Perigon) publish real, non-trial pricing in the **$50–$550/month** range for meaningful volume, and are dramatically cheaper than the enterprise OSINT platforms, though they cover news/web text rather than dark web, Telegram, or closed forums.

### Cited Findings
- **Webz.io**: Free plan is $0/month with $5 in monthly credits (Search & Open Web APIs, limited historical access, no card required). Paid "Scale" plan is pay-as-you-go/usage-based: Search API $0.001/call or $0.0005/result; Open Web Data Feeds $0.01/call or $0.005/result; Fact-check API $0.15/call; no minimum spend. "High Volume" tier is custom/contact-sales, offering bulk discounts, archive data up to 10 years, and tailored feeds. Coverage spans "170+ languages" across "news, blogs, discussions, and reviews," filterable by country/language/domain/source. — [Webz.io Pricing](https://webz.io/pricing/)
- **NewsCatcher API** (Web Search / CatchAll product): Individual tier is $0 pay-as-you-go (2-week search depth, 100 max results, 1 monitoring slot); Starter is **$50/month** for 6,000 credits (1-month search depth, 300 max results, up to 10 monitoring slots); Scale is **$500/month** for 60,000 credits (3-month depth, 1,000 max results, up to 20 slots); Enterprise is custom (full archive, unlimited results, 20+ slots). Unused credits roll over. Separately, NewsCatcher's "News API" product offers "structured news from 140k+ sources" with "7+ years of history" and full article text, but production access there is Enterprise-only with just a 7-day trial for lower tiers. — [NewsCatcher Pricing](https://www.newscatcherapi.com/pricing)
- **Event Registry / NewsAPI.ai**: Free plan — 2,000 search tokens, data from the last 30 days, no card required. "5K Plan" — **$90/month** for 5,000 tokens, covering ~500,000 articles from the last 30 days plus 100,000 historical searches back to 2014 (limited support). Token costs: 1 token per recent article search, 5 tokens per recent event search, 5 tokens per historical-search-year for articles, 20 tokens per historical-search-year for events; overage is $0.015/token. All plans include access to "150,000+ news outlets in 50+ languages," entity recognition/categorization, 99.99% uptime SLA, and REST/Python/Node SDKs. — [NewsAPI.ai Plans](https://newsapi.ai/plans)
- **Perigon**: Free plan — 150 requests/month, 1 req/sec, 3 months of historical data, real-time delivery, personal-use license only. Basic — **$250/month** (startup-eligible pricing) for 10,000 requests/month, 2 req/sec, 6 months history, 1-hour delayed delivery, Articles endpoint only, limited commercial license. Plus — **$550/month** (startup-eligible) for 50,000 requests/month, 4 req/sec, 3 years history, real-time delivery, access to Articles/Top Stories/Companies/People/Sources/Journalists endpoints, limited commercial license. Commercial — custom, starting at **$24,000/year**, with custom request limits, 10+ years history, full commercial license, custom sources, SLA, and dedicated support. — [Perigon API Pricing](https://perigon.io/products/pricing/apis)

### Inferences
- For a solo consultancy wanting broader/faster coverage than free tiers without enterprise-level spend, **NewsCatcher Starter ($50/mo)**, **Event Registry/NewsAPI.ai 5K Plan ($90/mo)**, and **Perigon Basic ($250/mo)** are realistic entry points that could feed a custom platform (like The Lens on Cloudflare Workers) via API — all three explicitly publish non-trial, production-usable pricing at low monthly cost, unlike every enterprise OSINT/social-listening vendor researched above.
- None of these four developer APIs appear to cover dark web, Telegram, or closed forums — they are news/web-article aggregators, not the broader OSINT/social-media surveillance category that Dataminr/Babel Street/Flashpoint/ShadowDragon occupy. A hybrid approach (cheap news API + free-tier social/Telegram scraping) may be the only financially realistic path for a solo African-security consultancy to broaden coverage beyond free sources.
- Perigon's and Event Registry's claimed 50,000+/150,000+ outlet coverage in 50+ languages, and Webz.io's 170+ languages, suggest reasonable potential for African-language source inclusion, though none of these sources explicitly confirm African-market depth (see gap below).

### Gaps
- Mediastack, Bing News Search/Azure, Google News/Alerts RSS, and Juicer.io were named in the assignment's suggested alternatives list but were **not researched** in this pass due to tool-call budget — their pricing and volume limits remain unconfirmed and should be covered separately if needed.
- None of the four developer APIs' documentation was checked specifically for African-language or African-outlet source counts — this is a meaningful gap given Afrilens's stated focus and should be verified directly against each vendor's source-coverage docs or source-list exports.
- Perigon and NewsCatcher's historical-data/startup-eligibility pricing (e.g., "eligible startups" qualification for Perigon's $250/$550 tiers) was not clarified — it's unclear whether a one-person consultancy would qualify for those rates or pay a higher standard-commercial rate instead.
